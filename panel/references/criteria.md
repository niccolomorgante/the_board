## 1. Scored elements

Rows follow the manuscript, because that is how referees read and how findings are written.
Keep the labels short — they have to fit a column in the panel score matrix.

| Element | What a referee is looking for |
|---|---|
| Framing | Is the question worth answering, is the gap real, and is the objective stated precisely enough to be met or missed? |
| Methods | Do the design, data and analysis answer the question that was asked? |
| Results | Is what was found reported completely, and read without inflation? |
| Discussion | Do the conclusions stay inside what the results support? |
| Clarity | Can a reader outside the authors' subfield state the contribution after one pass? Scored on the whole document, not a section. |

**Magnitude is not scored — it is a finding.** Whether the effect, at the size reported,
would change what anyone actually does belongs in prose under Discussion, from the reviewer
positioned to judge it. Say what would have to change to move a decision, and if nothing
would, say what magnitude would.

Adjust rows to the field: strong methodological traditions usually split Methods into
design and analysis; qualitative work replaces Methods and Results with rigour and
reflexivity. Add a row only alongside a reviewer who can actually score it.

**Findings are ordered by these rows, in this order.** A finding names its location before
it names its objection — section, page, or figure. An unlocated criticism cannot be acted
on and triage will cut it.

## 2. How review actually works, calibrated to this manuscript's own field

No single venue is configured here. Before scoring, infer the field and the likely venue
tier from what the manuscript itself signals — its citations, its scope, its polish, its
stated audience — and calibrate to a realistic referee at that tier, not to the most
rigorous review this paper could hypothetically be given. A student thesis chapter, a
field journal submission, and a flagship-journal submission earn different referees with
different patience, and treating all three as the last one manufactures severity that
was never earned.

- **Referee count and pool.** Most venues run 2–3 referees, not one omniscient reader. You
  are one seat — say what you are positioned to catch, not everything a committee might
  catch collectively.
- **Reading load and turnaround.** A referee reads this once, competently, on a deadline —
  not line by line with unlimited time. A finding that only surfaces on a forensic reread
  is not a realistic referee's finding; downgrade it or move it to "what I could not judge."
- **Revision culture.** Most venues send a technically sound paper with fixable gaps to
  MAJOR REVISION. REJECT means a different paper is needed, not that the MAJOR list is
  long — the count of MAJOR findings does not by itself justify REJECT.
- **A real referee rations objections.** Raising everything defensible is not more rigorous
  than raising what is decision-relevant; it is how a referee earns a reputation for being
  unreasonable, which costs them influence with editors. Before including a finding, ask
  whether you would actually spend one of your limited objections on it if you knew the
  editor was counting.

## 3. Scoring scale

- **3** — no objection. A referee would let this through as it stands.
- **2** — a named weakness, fixable in revision. Every 2 must carry a finding saying what
  to fix.
- **1** — a weakness that revision alone does not fix. New data, new analysis, or a
  narrower claim.

Do not invent halves.
